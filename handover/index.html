Keystone handover

The discharge bridge.

The pharmacist still decides.

This is the path from a hospital ward to a nursing home or a community pharmacy. It is not the community counter, and it is not a hospital dispensing system. The counter page stays the counter. This page is the short bridge after discharge.

Keystone, the counter.↗
01

The problem.

The ward chart, the discharge chart, and the bag do not always say the same thing.

A nursing home and a community pharmacy need to know what was ordered, what actually left, and whether a packed aid will be ready. Re-keying that by phone, while the patient is already in a taxi, is the tax.

Keystone puts the mismatch in front of a pharmacist. It does not dispense, and it does not turn the chart into a prescription.

02

Three records, kept apart.

Orders

The discharge chart holds the orders for a short bridge, and the last dose given. It is not a prescription. Nothing is dispensed from it.

Hospital supply

Which items left, how many, and that they are original packs. A quantity is a numeral and words. If the words are missing, the share is blocked.

Packed aid

Whether the community pharmacy was told, whether its acceptance cut-off had passed, and whether delivery is expected.

03

What happens on the bridge.

  1. 01Pharmacist

    Both charts are present.

    The ward chart and the discharge chart are read together. The discharge chart is the short bridge, not a script.

  2. 02Keystone surfaces

    Last dose given.

    Every order needs a last dose. A missing time is flagged. It is not guessed.

  3. 03Keystone surfaces

    Supply, in numeral and words.

    What left the hospital, and how many. Missing words block the share. A mismatch between the numeral and the words is flagged, not repaired.

  4. 04Site rule

    Cut-offs belong to the site.

    Each site sets the hospital pharmacy cut-off and the time a community pharmacy will still accept a pack. The starting rule used here is 5:00 pm and 3:30 pm. A later skeleton is a site override, not a new product.

  5. 05Keystone surfaces

    An order with no matching supply.

    The line is flagged. The brief can say the pharmacy declined. A person confirms the brief arrived, or the record shows it did not.

  6. 06Pharmacist decides

    Schedule 8 is its own track.

    An S8 on the chart and not in the bag is flagged. A discharge after the site cut-off is flagged. The wording is that local policy and timing can stop it travelling. The brief does not say the law stopped it.

  7. 07Keystone surfaces

    High-risk gaps only.

    Missing supply is flagged for opioids, anticoagulants, levodopa, and insulin. No substitute is suggested.

  8. 08Keystone surfaces

    A withheld medicine needs a reason.

    If the reason is missing, it is flagged. The reason is not invented.

  9. 09Site rule

    How long the bridge is.

    One day when quick supply is assured. Otherwise three days. The chart has to say that quick supply is assured. If it does not, the longer bridge stands.

  10. 10Pharmacist decides

    The brief is read, then a person acts.

    Keystone does not prescribe, dispense, or counsel in their place.

04

Local by design.

The chart photo stays on the machine that holds the record.

It is not placed on a public control panel. The current retention rule is seven years, then the photo is deleted.

The model does not get to change the record. If a fact is not written there, the brief says it does not know. A correction is a new line. You can see what was written.

Nothing on this page is a claim that local storage blocks a foreign law. The fence is simpler: the record stays where the pharmacists can open it.

05

What this is, and is not.

It is

  • A discharge bridge beside the Keystone counter, not a replacement for it.
  • Private research in Adelaide.
  • A sketch of flags a pharmacist can refuse.
  • A synthetic reconciliation, on paper, from a long-stay inpatient chart onto a drafted layout.

It is not

  • A hospital dispensing system, and not the community counter.
  • The official discharge form. The real form may still name its boxes differently.
  • Regulated clinical decision support. It does not diagnose, prescribe, dispense, or treat.
  • A live ScriptCheck, AMH, MINFOS, or hospital-record integration.
  • Clinical advice. The synthetic exercise is not for clinical use.
