01The problem

Some days, the queue wins.

Community pharmacies have a morning rush, a midday rush, and an evening rush. The legal sequence is long. Under pressure, steps get skipped. A careful pharmacist still has days when the queue wins. That is burnout, not a missing gadget.

01Today

Re-key ScriptCheck

Log in to ScriptCheck separately, then type the patient in again. The same person, entered twice, while the queue waits.

02Today

S8 interval by hand

A Schedule 8 script can carry an interval: it cannot be dispensed again inside a set period. Today that interval is worked out by hand, mid-queue.

03Today

History in your head

AMH lookup, new-medicine counsel, stock and S8 recording, all carried in memory. Every lookup takes the pharmacist's eyes off the patient.

Keystone is designed to take the lookup and re-keying tax so counsel is what remains. It does not skip a legal S8 record. It does not dispense.

04Local by design

One server,
and it is the pharmacy's.

All patient information is designed to remain on the pharmacy's local server. It does not leave that server for model training or a vendor memory product.

The graph, the stills, the checks and the sources behind them stay where the dispensing happens.

Stays on the local server
  • The patient file
  • Stills of scripts
  • Interaction checks and the sources behind them
  • That pharmacy's own inventory history
  • The licensed AMH copy
Does not leave for
  • Model training
  • A vendor memory product
05Model-agnostic

Memory stays ours.
Models are interchangeable.

The model is reasoning capacity. The graph is the record. Either can be upgraded without touching the other.

The model

Reasoning capacity.

Rented for what it can work out today. Rated, swapped, upgraded. It holds none of the record.

The graph

The record.

Owned by the pharmacy and kept on its server. Every answer points back into it. Change the model and the graph does not move.
